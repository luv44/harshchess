SMARTER TEACHING ALGORITHM:
1. Compare the engine's pre-move MultiPV candidates with the played move and opponent continuation at comparable budgets. Normalize mate/WDL and centipawn evidence; avoid strong error labels from shallow analysis.
2. Produce a verified FactPacket with before/after FEN, legal move, opponent threat, candidate lines, confidence, motif and relevant learner history. Teach what to notice, why the move worked/failed, why not the alternative and a legal counterfactual reply. Optional LLM may explain checked facts in the player's chosen language, never invent moves or scores; cache and cap paid requests with local fallback.
3. Diagnose only supported missed checks/captures/threats, hanging pieces, shallow calculation, poor trade, king safety, opening/endgame gaps or UNKNOWN. Never guess private player intent.
4. Track micro-skills separately for recognition, calculation, move choice, execution and transfer. Start uncertain; update from independent attempts, recency-weighted retests and own-game evidence. One move never proves mastery; keep evidence behind each score.
5. Rank exercises after filtering illegal, duplicate, too-hard or low-quality positions. Starting score, with signals normalized 0..1: .23 weakness + .18 recentMistake + .16 informationGain + .13 ownGameRelevance + .11 difficultyFit + .09 forgettingRisk + .06 transfer + .04 novelty - repetitionPenalty. Treat weights as tunable, not proven science; aim for about 70–85% expected success, then adjust to outcomes.
6. Give a stepwise hint, concrete lesson and next move; later retest the same idea in a different position. Show improvement only from repeated comparable attempts; otherwise say “not enough data.” Teach sequences, not isolated puzzles.
7. Start with a short optional diagnostic, or infer cautiously from play. Maintain a per-skill proficiency estimate AND uncertainty; separate unaided correct decisions from hinted guesses. Use item difficulty, outcome, repeated attempts and time context as evidence, but never equate fast moves alone with understanding. Prioritize high-uncertainty, important skills for diagnostic questions.
8. Build a prerequisite concept graph (e.g., noticing checks -> defending threats -> calculating forcing lines). Do not assign an advanced combination before its necessary ideas are understood. Choose candidate lessons by the scoring rule above, then diversity-rerank by motif, opening/endgame, source and recent repetition. Retain a small controlled exploration share to discover unseen weaknesses; keep difficulty near the student's learning edge.
9. For spaced revision, store last unaided success, confidence, interval and observed recall. Schedule a review when estimated recall falls below a configurable threshold; shorten intervals after failure and lengthen only after independent success. Recalculate from actual attempts and allow breaks without punitive streak logic. Do not manufacture a precise mastery percentage from insufficient data.
10. Mine a player's OWN games for teachable critical moments: missed threats, changes in mate/WDL, practical alternatives and repeated error patterns. Use legal replay and bounded engine verification; reject noisy low-confidence positions. Show a short counterfactual consequence and then generate a different transfer position to test the same concept.
11. Adapt explanation depth and hint order to level and selected language. Prefer one observation, one reason and one next action; expose deeper analysis on demand. Ask “What were you considering?” only as an optional self-report, then compare the answer with board facts. Track whether the learner understood on a later attempt, not whether they merely clicked a hint.
12. Run calibration checks: compare predicted success/recall with actual results by skill and difficulty, adjust weights/difficulty slowly, and detect repeated or misleading lessons. Document algorithm inputs, evidence, version and limits so an improvement report can be audited.
13. Make a TeachingOpportunityFilter before coaching: ask whether the position has a clear, engine-verified learning point, whether the chosen language has a trustworthy explanation, and whether it differs from recent lessons. If not, give concise move feedback rather than manufacturing a dramatic mistake. Rank by expected learning value under time, device and user-attention budgets. Never turn routine moves into excessive AI calls.
14. When analysis is unstable, run a bounded second check on the critical candidate or show uncertainty; allow the coach to abstain. Distinguish a forced tactic from a stylistic engine preference, and avoid penalizing several similarly sound moves. Each diagnosis and candidate explanation must link back to the exact position and legal continuation that support it.
15. Plan a personal learning path using prerequisite skills, most repeated own-game mistakes, due spaced reviews and the user's daily time goal. A short daily session should mix one due review, one own-game lesson and one transfer test if evidence supports them. Add a meaningful Daily Boss position and weekly improvement report only after the corresponding data and tests exist; streaks must remain optional and non-punitive.
16. For each suggested alternative, replay the actual legal variation from the exact FEN, keep evaluation from the same player's perspective and stop a lesson when opponent replies overturn its claim. If comparable candidate evaluations overlap or the advantage depends on a fragile horizon, group them as reasonable choices instead of marking an error. Distinguish board fact, engine preference and learner inference in the FactPacket.
17. Measure teaching usefulness through delayed, unaided transfer attempts in different positions. Compare predicted and observed learning outcomes within each skill/difficulty band; tune ranking and hint timing gradually while keeping a fixed baseline and uncertainty intervals. Give learners a way to mark feedback as wrong; quarantine bad positions and recheck them before reuse.
18. Make coaching effort adaptive to device, position and learner: instant legal move + one useful question, short bounded analysis when a critical position warrants it, and optional deeper analysis on request. Cap battery/CPU/cloud budgets; pause workers offscreen; prefer verified rule-based language templates when translation or engine confidence is low. No false precision and no paid-model call required for every move.
